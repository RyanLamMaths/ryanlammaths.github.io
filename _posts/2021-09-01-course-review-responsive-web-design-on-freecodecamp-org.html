How could a man unable to draw well got a certificate in web design?

As a student who is passionate about presenting different ideas, whether academic or not, I think there is increasing importance on learning how technology can help me. One idea that comes up to me is learning about website architecture. Although I am currently using WordPress, which provides me with a useful template, I still feel that it is crucial to learn the theory behind designing a website.

I was a bit hesitant before starting to work on this, as I knew my art and aesthetic sense is as terrible as it is… and I am pretty afraid that it might affect my ability to comprehend different visual elements in a webpage.

After taking the course, needless to say, I had understood more about how to interpret each visual style that is present in a website by reading its code. It teaches the basics of different elements on a website with the usage of HTML5 and CSS. It is a course heavily focus on visual design, including topics like increasing accessibility in a website, arranging different elements, and the new but powerful CSS flexbox.

I like the coding environment presented in the course, and since it is completely free (unlike other competitors which require a paid service often), I think that the content covered is vast and sufficient compared to other coding courses. However, it is quite a bit of an overestimate to say that it contains 300 hours of content (as stated in the certificate). Consider how I finished the course under 3 weeks… but maybe it is the projects / final assignments that take up most of the time, I will explain that later.

The course contains numerous programming “challenges” that let you solve to gain an understanding of a subject. Each unit has about 20 of them. Disappointingly, these “challenges” usually contain only 1 to 2 lines of code, with very explicit instructions, so most of the time it is just reading comprehension for me. However, some of it might be challenging for coding newbies. So sometimes I am feeling that I am overqualified for the certificate.

After the challenges, there are 5 final tasks to finish to claim the certification. To finish the task, you must write a website, fulfilling the user stories set by the questions, and pass all the tests on a test checker.  For each task, you can see the test checker’s result and show what you did wrong. You also have an example website (which pass all the tests) for you to refer to. I like both of the features, it makes website design seems less boring and much more creative, it also can boost you to design a more appealing website if you are able.

I understand that most of our fellow learners here would want to write a beautiful website, but sadly I am running out of time when I was studying for this certificate. It is September, and I had to prepare my materials back for my uni, and as mentioned, I am also not good with visual arts. I ended up writing a minimal example with the least material while passing all the tests. And that gives me a huge time save, I don’t know whether this is a good thing or not, but it passes all the tests so I don’t have a lot to say about it.

Criticisms aside, the content still makes me confident in the basics of designing a website. Does it make me think it is an artsy thing? Maybe, that’s why it is a 3.5/5 from me, but I would recommend this course to people who have absolutely no programming experience and want to learn a new skill. And most importantly, it is completely free! Just go to freecodecamp.org and get started!
