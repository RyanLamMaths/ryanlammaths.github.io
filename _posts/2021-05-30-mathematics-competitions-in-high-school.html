I studied high school in Hong Kong with Physics, Chemistry and “Further Mathematics” (M2 in HKDSE) as electives. So I was probably the most generic “scientist nerdy” type of people you would find in school, not particularly able or whatsoever. However, I did have an immense interest in mathematics, especially after experiencing outside lessons such as EPYMT. 

That said, I did not participate in any mathematics competition during high school, nor I participated in any kind of competition training. This might seem odd, but even now, I am still convinced that I made the right decision by not participating in any at that time. The idea of constantly participating in mathematics competition is doing more harm than good for me.

Schoolwork and Pressure

The current Hong Kong’s education system (the HKDSE) is infamous for its competitiveness and difficulty. Students are, if not obligated, expected to cram things in 3 years. For comparison, this is equivalent to absorbing everything you learn in GCSE and A-Levels while having a year less. We have had extra lessons in the summer holidays, after school, only to do a few more practice papers and tests (and my secondary school is already known for not pushing students too hard).

That means after a day’s work you will be exhausted, and given how much thinking and brainpower you would need for mathematics competition questions, it wasn’t the best idea for me to stumble into these. Besides, I was already participating in different pre-university math classes and reading math books already, which I would argue to be more effective.

The idea behind Math Competitions

Some people might say I am just being lazy and don’t want to work harder. While it might be true that I can achieve more, I still don’t think mathematics competitions would be the right way. To be honest, I think having participated in a competition doesn’t help very much in studying mathematics as one could have expected. 

This Answer from Quora made a point that I agree with: although the answer is specifically talking about the International Mathematics Olympiad (IMO), I think it applies to all mathematics competition at a high school level. The answer to a question requires a trick and/or a key observation to get through, and it could get quite frustrating when you try your best, but you don’t succeed. (I have included a reference here, please tell me that I am funny)

Anyways, doing mathematics as a university major is more than the skills you need in a competition. Sure, practicing would help you in finding patterns and identifying ideas. But more importantly, doing mathematics requires the ability to invent new ideas, communicate, and then present. This can only be trained by researching and presenting, in my opinion, since you don’t have a strict time limit by then, and can explore as deep as you like. I had experienced this full learning cycle during my Mathematical Investigation Workshop, and I can conclude that it is nothing like competition training.

My findings can be best summarized by a quote from the answer I mentioned:

The plight of the IMO Olympian is like that of a child at Easter Egg hunts, where they have grown used to finding pretty eggs laid out previously by adults, and then when they grow up and come to a large grassy field, they are frustrated, because no matter how hard they try they can’t find a single egg in the bushes —because in the real world of mathematics, there is no adult planting pretty eggs for them to find.

Epilogue/Disclaimer

I am not saying Competition mathematics is not a good activity for high school student, I think you are confident enough, things would turn out great for you and you will have the bragging rights too! However, I want to emphasize that mathematics competitions are quite different from mathematics itself, and not all good mathematicians are good competition problem-solver, vice versa.

After saying this much, I want to declare that I am also on a university mathematics competition training, so that’s why I have a little bit of experience of what high school students are experiencing in mathematical competitions.

Also, starting from now on, I would post some explanations of competitions problems here, showing as much thought process that I had gone through as possible.
