I still remember the mix of excitement and nervousness when I stepped foot in Bristol for the first time. Before the moment I received my study abroad offer (to the University of Arizona), I would never expect the feeling to hit me for the second time, but it sure did.

At Bristol: The Coding Club

Earlier in 2022, when I was at Bristol, I organized the coding club. Where I helped with all sorts of programming questions. It has been founded by two study skills advocates after realizing STEM students receive little to no continuous support when a course requires heavy coding. This had put a heavy toll on students that are not familiar with it.

As such, every Monday and Thursday, we have a 2-hour peer support session at one
of the study centres, where student advocates will help in answering all sorts of programming questions (Python, R, Java…) to the best of their abilities.

During the second semester, since there is a programming course which is compulsory
for all mathematics students, I signed up as an advocate who regularly helps with the Coding Club.

At one point, I decided to reach the lecturer of our programming course, and have him promote the Coding Club on our course website. And since then, there are more and more people coming to the study centre. At one point, I have to help 16 people with their math homework! In my opinion, this is a fun and valuable experience. I learned more about how to take an active role when organizing peer support activities. I also learned to lead and cultivate discussions throughout different sessions.

At Arizona: My Fall Semester

However, my life in the US is worlds apart. Although I had a rough idea of what will be coming ahead, experiencing the graduate courses, the homework, and the workplace culture first-hand would still put me in shock and huge pressure.

I feel way more challenged as the courses push me to the limits than I was in the UK. In the UK, my understanding comes from writing my notes and reading extra materials. But now, since the graduate courses have the content presented differently, I need to rely on homework and talking to professors to gain intuition into them.

I have only been here for a few months, but I can already imagine the struggles I will have as a potential US/UK PhD student in mathematics. Nevertheless, I am still glad that I can get a head start to experience the shock early.

Outside of Studying at Arizona

With this in mind, I ended up joining the Western Algebraic Geometry Symposium (WAGS) in Riverside, California. It turns out to be one of the most inspiring academic events I have joined. Despite not understanding 90% of the content, I managed to get a “feel” of Algebraic Geometry. I also found some topics that piqued my interest, and some kind-hearted PhD students explained their projects to me.  I also got to know some big names who are currently working on this hot and ever-expanding subject.

Outside of my studies, I also have leisure time as my other stream of inspiration. This year, I spent most of it on different sports, such as hiking, functional training and climbing. It let me realize how important it is to have undivided attention when pursuing a task, and how to listen to my body when things go south. Coincidentally, as a mathematician, I would also argue this is very important when tackling difficulties in research.

(This is a photo of me trying climbing and bouldering for the first time in Tucson.)

Conclusion

Compared to the UK, I would say I spent way more time in the US stumbling and exploring new and unknown territories. Moreover, I feel like I am a step closer to what mathematical research really is: pushing boundaries with careful justifications. As time goes on, I feel like I am slowly but surely adapting to the working style. My trip to the US definitely pushed my boundaries in understanding and practising mathematics. I sincerely hope the spring semester will be as fruitful as the fall one!
