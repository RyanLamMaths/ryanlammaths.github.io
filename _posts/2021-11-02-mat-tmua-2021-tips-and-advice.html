Hey all! It is MAT tomorrow (and TMUA the day after), I guess everyone is quite nervous about it. As a student who took the MAT before, I just want to offer some tips and advice that might help! These strategies might not work for anyone, but here are some of them I think test-preppers can take some references on:

0️⃣MAT is hard, TMUA is hard, math is hard!

Firstly, I really want to congratulate you guys on making it towards the end of an exam prep period. It is no easy task to surround yourself with so many difficult questions for such a long time.⏲️ Each question requires some serious thinking, and it is frustrating when the results don’t click! But don’t worry, those admission tests are designed like that: the examiners want to see you struggle, and want to stretch your limits😈. So don’t be let down because you hadn’t got any clue about a question! Remember, the mean score of applicants who were made an Oxford offer could be as low as 60/100 in MAT, and you never know when the question is getting exceptionally hard!😫

1️⃣Golden Rule: ⭐Don’t panic!⭐

This is much easier said than done, it will affect your university applications by a lot, and you have prepared so much, so it is completely normal to feel tense. Especially when you are in an exam hall: all the people are focused and looking at their paper ferociously, it is easy to fall into a state of panic after starting. One thing that will be nice to remember, is that if you are panicking on a question, then probably the people around you do too. So you are not alone in this sense. Think about what you had practised, skip the question if necessary and keep following your strategy in doing the question!

2️⃣Develop a strategy📰

By now, you probably had practised a few papers and know that the marking scheme is: 4×10 MCs + 15×4 Long Questions that make a total of 100 marks in 150 minutes. Think about approximately how long you are spending on each question. I made an approximate timetable📝 before I start working (approx. half an hour in 5 MCs, then 20 minutes in each long question), then follow it throughout. Think about will you check your work at all or the end of your question? Or after completing as much as you could? It is okay either way, but make sure you are clear about what to do, so you make full use of your time!

3️⃣Work quickly but write a lot!✏️

The time limit for MAT is especially harsh, once you laid your eyes on a question you almost had to start right away in order to get the answer. For every question (especially long questions), my strategy is to write as many things as possible when I could think of any. Especially in long questions, if you don’t write any of your thoughts down, then the markers are not going to know your thinking process😖! Even if your thought process is wrong, there is a very high chance to get partial credit by noting down any pattern that emerges from the question. Be confident! Don’t be afraid to make any mistakes!

4️⃣Claim what you are going to do.🔊

You won’t believe how useful it is to have phrases like “comparing coefficients in the polynomial…”, “solving the simultaneous equations…”, “rearranging…”, “using the substitution…” weaved into your answer! It makes your presentation way clearer than just writing one line and then the other. This is quite hard to do especially you are panicking, but if you can realize what you are doing by writing such phrases, it can make your answers less error-prone✔️!

5️⃣Write Clearly and legibly.

You all probably have heard from others how important handwriting is, so I am not going to elaborate a lot here🥱. But one common misconception is that in Multiple choice questions (MCQs) you can just write in a language that only you understands. Although you don’t have to write phrases to guide the markers, it would still be a good thing to do if you can write as if you are answering a long question (if you have the time), again, this highly reduced the likeliness of making a careless mistake, since you are so clear with your work!

6️⃣Double-check your workings!🧐

It is really up to different people on when they will check their workings. For me, after a painful expansion of brackets or taking out minus signs, I always look twice and stop for a few seconds to make sure that I am doing the right thing. Some might do that after they are stuck or after they finish a whole (sub-)question. Those strategies are fine as well👍! There is no one successful way, but just do the way that you are most comfortable with.

7️⃣Sleep Well, eat your breakfast!🥣

It is very important to have some energy before tackling such a hard paper⚡! But of course, it is definitely not the case that everyone can just sleep once they lay down on their bed, especially before an exam day like this😪. So, maybe read something, do something that you like before sleep, talk to friends, play some games, make yourself at ease the night before😸! And of course, check your alarm, make yourself feel comfortable after you wake up, enjoy the day, and enjoy the paper!

🏁Final words

There are only 14 questions in MAT that you need to attempt, so sometimes luck plays a role here in whether the light bulb in your head💡 suddenly clicks. Your performance is only an indicator of your ability, but it is certainly not what DESCRIBES your abilities. No matter how the exam turned out, I am sure all of you reading this had put a lot of effort into it, and you should be proud of yourself🥺! To all of the candidates here, I wish you could do your very best, and best of luck💪! Do feel free if you have any questions or need any help, I will try my best to help and provide support if you need to.
