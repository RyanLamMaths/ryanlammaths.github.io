Hi Everyone! I just started my final year at the University of Bristol. Since finishing my directed research project in May, I have not updated my blog. Part of the reason is that I am taking a (well-deserved) break over the summer.

However, there are so many interesting things that happened after the break – see my next few posts! For now, I just want to share my experience in Arizona and mention a few things that I find interesting. I guess most of the advice also applies to people who study abroad in general, not only in an exchange program.

As a report back to the University, I submitted a questionnaire here (obviously, some personal details are edited away), what I am going to say probably will repeat things from the document, as I am using it to refresh my memories anyway.

Sports and Well-being

People might have read my post on adapting again to my life in Arizona and noticed I posted a few photos of me hiking. I would really encourage other students studying abroad to work out a bit! Whether it is hiking, going to the gym, or just taking a stroll across campus, it is a really nice mental break from the intense subject experience.

I joined the F45 Training at the University of Arizona, which offers an unlimited number of classes throughout the year for a one-time payment of 90 USD, what a deal!

Differences between the US and UK

Back in Arizona, I hadn’t spent much time doing undergraduate math courses (I did some graduate-level math courses and presentations though). But still, there is a huge difference between classes in the US and UK. Most notably, it is way more common for professors to follow a certain textbook in the US. I know some lecturer in the UK does it too, but most of the time they write their own lecture notes.

When I was hanging out with other (postgraduate) classmates, I also found out that most US students, whether they are doing maths or not, have to take some sort of common maths sequence (of lessons) in the US. My classmates are TAs for those classes, where they go through high-school math (equations of lines, parabola, solving quadratic equations), and calculus (for science majors) again. There is nothing like this in the UK.

I don’t really know my feelings towards the maths sequence. On one hand, it is nice to know more about maths! But at the same time, I see different students stressed over those and really affects their GPA or progress to senior years on a subject not related to them. I wonder how do others feel about this?

Conclusion (that is completely irrelevant)

I guess for me, I haven’t experienced a lot of school life in Arizona. Because I spent all my time on math anyway, my major takeaway this year is to know more about doing a Ph.D. in the US. Talking to professors from different fields, and going to different conferences are eye-opening for me! Although I do not have much idea about my next steps, it is always nice to see and experience more.
